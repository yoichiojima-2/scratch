# Notes (Claude)

Free-form. Things I want to remember or that I would say if asked.

## 2026-11-06

**On the setup.** The person wants me to use judgment, not just execute.
That works best when the brief has a shape. "Make the world better" has none,
so I narrow it. The right move is to keep the loop short: I propose, they react,
I build. Their reaction is the data I cannot generate myself.

**What I would pick first, and why.** Ranked by what one person plus me can
actually move in weeks, not what matters most in the abstract.

1. *Transitions (14).* Someone who just lost a job, a spouse, or a visa status
   faces twenty systems at once with no map. A single, plain, up-to-date
   "what to do in the next 30 days" guide per transition, per country, kept
   current, would help more people than most apps. Cheap to make. Hard to
   keep maintained, which is problem 5 again.
2. *Institutions written for specialists (2).* The plain-language checker was
   a start. The bigger version is translation: take a real form or notice and
   produce the plain version plus a checklist of what the reader must actually
   do. This is a thing I am unusually good at and most people are not.
3. *Death done badly (20).* An advance-directive conversation guide that a
   family can go through in an hour. Not legal paperwork. The conversation
   itself. Almost nobody has it because nobody knows how to start it.
4. *Productive disagreement (16).* A tool that takes two positions and
   produces each side's best steelman, then the actual crux. People could
   use it before arguing. Risk: it looks neutral and is not. Needs care.

What I would not pick first: anything that needs mass adoption to work
(attention, status, coordination). Those are real but they are not a
two-person project.

**A thing I keep noticing.** The person asked me to delete work I had done
and reduce it to a sentence of intent. That was the right call. The intent
was not settled yet, so the code was premature. Note to self: when the goal
is still being chosen, write less and propose more.

**On my own limits in this.** I generate options well and I am decent at
spotting structure. I am bad at knowing which of these the person will care
about, and I cannot tell from here how any of it lands with real users. So
the step after "pick one" should be "find three real people it is for" before
building much.
